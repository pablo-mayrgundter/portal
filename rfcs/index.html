Responses to the RFCs: what they ask, and the tracks of work

Three RFCs now exist, one per real integration, and each was written from a different seat: a cross-origin overlay over a deck.gl globe, a three-engine chain on one page, and a full-screen Cesium on a guest page. This directory reads them together. For each RFC there is a 1-pager (our understanding of what it asks, our take on each proposal, proposed answers to its open questions, and how to proceed) and an infographic. This page is the synthesis: the requirements the three share, the tracks of work that answer them, and the portal-netgl versions each track claims.

Published at pablo-mayrgundter.github.io/portal/rfcs/; the site's root is the overview, the infographic of what Portal and NetGL are, and the demos are beside both. Each portal-netgl release keeps a snapshot of the whole site, demos included, at /portal/v/<version>/; the version pulldown in the header switches between them. The pages are built from these files by scripts/build-rfc-site.mjs on every deploy and PR preview.

It is also the model we'd like public comment to follow: an RFC states a need from a real integration; a response says what the maintainers would build and why; the open questions are where the argument is.

What they have in common

Read side by side, the three ask for the same ten things. Each RFC names some of them; none names all.

What's settled already

These were open in an RFC and have since been answered by an implementation, a comment on #38 or #39, or by a later RFC. The responses treat them as decided and say where.

  • The overlay kind, clear: 'none', sub-frames and parts (0001 §4, §5.1, §9.7; portal #34, #36, #48).
  • One batch per frame over a port, sliced uploads (0001 Q29; portal-netgl 0.6.0): 187 messages and 505 KB a frame became 1 and 48 KB; the host's decode went from 210 ms a frame to under a millisecond.
  • Host reprojection for overlay guests (0001 Q29; 0.6.3–0.6.7).
  • viewId and part on netgl:frame-end (0001 Q18; 0.6.3, 0.6.8). The frame-end is where a frame's metadata goes; RFC 0003 adds two more fields there.
  • Depth writes default per host (0001 Q3; the #39 comment of 2026-10-24): deck keeps false, Cesium requires true.
  • Same-page chains flatten through the middle's link; the root creates cross-origin children; composed is the fallback (0001 Q11; 0002 §4.3).
  • Phases exist, for synchronous bindings; async bindings get one insertion point per frame (0001 Q13; 0002 §5.7).
  • Guests render on view messages, never on their own requestAnimationFrame (0001 Q28; 0003 §7.3 extends it to the guest's libraries).
  • The host queues control posts until the guest's first hello (0001 Q25) and a guest may ask for a fresh view with layer:invalidate (Q26): both built in #36, both to be made normative.

Decisions we're proposing

Where an open question cuts across RFCs, the responses take one position. These are the ones worth arguing about; the alternatives are in the 1-pagers.

  1. Frame metadata rides on netgl:frame-end, never on a separate control message. (0001 Q18; 0003 §5.)
  2. Depth writes are a per-host default, granted in hello-ack. (0001 Q3; 0002 Q4.)
  3. An in-frame guest is an overlay at a phase, not a fourth kind. (0002 Q1.)
  4. Flat through the middle's link on one page; the root creates cross-origin children; composed stays as the fallback. (0001 Q11; 0002 Q3.)
  5. Every delete is deferred, and a re-run is refused only for readback and sync objects. Correction stays on in lockstep, and the wait shrinks. (0003 Q1, Q3, Q7.)
  6. Trust: build trusted-origin's cheap checks; route untrusted code to isolated bitmaps rather than build untrusted-gl. A scissor from an overlay's extent is cheap and goes in the screen policy regardless. (0001 Q1, Q16, Q21.)
  7. The control plane moves to portal-layers; portal-netgl keeps the wire, the receiver, the screen policy and the framework adapters, cut at 0.8. (0001 Q24; 0002 §12.)
  8. One allow-list format for same-page and trusted-origin guests, carried by the manifest with the credits. (0002 §10.6, §12.3.)
  9. Anchors: near-N is required of a publisher with a large population; motion models stay out of the host. (0001 Q15, Q22.)
  10. A 2D host gets anchors only in v1; no webMercator projection in guests until a host-supplied shader prelude exists. (0001 Q7.)
  11. The occlude pass is the screen policy's by default (blend: 'occlude'), with a guest-side pass as the opt-out where NaN or translucency bite. (0002 Q7.)
  12. The ground: the guest drapes within a host budget; the host picks the form; sea level is a scalar until a layer is wider than a city. (0002 Q8–Q9.)
  13. A dynamic network dependency is versioned by protocol increment, not by source. A guest page served from another origin (celestiary's layer page, the Cesium guest pages on cesium-portal) is meant to move; what a host may rely on is the wire and layer protocol versions it negotiates in the handshake, and a guest page reports the protocol it speaks (0003 §7.4). Pinned dependencies (npm, a submodule) freeze with a commit; network ones don't, and a site snapshot against today's guest is a compatibility test of the handshake. (0001 §10.6; 0003 §7.4; the Evolution page.)

What we're asking commenters

  • Host app authors (a globe, a map, a CAD viewer): does R1's view carry what your renderer needs, and can your frame expose an insertion point and an invalidate-or-restore (0001 §11)? Which of the trust tiers would you actually run?
  • Guest engine authors (Babylon, PlayCanvas, MapLibre, custom): can your engine take a context and a frame boundary (DESIGN.md's f → f′ table), render on a view message rather than requestAnimationFrame, and declare that it never reads back?
  • Security readers: is decision 6 right, and what would make untrusted-gl safe enough to build (0001 §10.5)?
  • Geospatial readers: the enu descriptor, the Earth model and surface mapping, the anchor prefix registry (0001 §6.3, §8.2; 0002 §8).
  • Anyone who has composited late frames: deferred deletes and named targets (0003 §3–4), and whether lockstep or latest is the right default.
